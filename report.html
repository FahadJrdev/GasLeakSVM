The report, chapter by chapter

The report has front matter and five chapters. Here is what each part says, why it is there, and the one point from it an examiner is most likely to test.

In plain words

The report tells one story in five steps. Why gas leaks matter (Chapter 1). What others have tried and what is missing (Chapter 2). How your method works, with all the mathematics (Chapter 3). What happened when you ran it (Chapter 4). And what it means, including its limits (Chapter 5).

If you can say one sentence for each chapter, you can survive the opening of any viva. Those sentences are in the box at the bottom of this page.

Front matter

Title page, abstract, acknowledgements, certification, declaration, table of contents, list of 19 tables, list of 22 figures and a list of abbreviations. The abstract already contains every headline number: 80.31% → 98.23%, F1 0.9823, AUC 0.9986, 99.03% sensitivity, about 1.1 ms per reading.

1.1
Why it matters: ILO figures (2.78 million work deaths a year) and real accidents — Bhopal, LG Polymers, Shanghai and Alabama ammonia cold stores, and in Bangladesh the BM Container Depot (51 dead), Seema Oxygen plant, Siddique Bazar and Narayanganj.

Table 1: safety limits (OSHA, NIOSH, IDLH, LEL) for LPG, methane, CO, H₂S, ammonia, hydrogen and benzene.
1.3
Causes of leaks: equipment failure, corrosion, storage and temperature failure, cylinder handling, illegal connections, human error.

Sensor types; how a metal-oxide (MOS) sensor works; Table 2 lists the seven MQ sensors.
1.5–1.6
Figure 1 taxonomy of detection methods; why threshold alarms fail; what "real-time" means.
1.7
Four objectives: derive the SVM, build the model, evaluate it, test real-time speed and drift.

Most likely question

"Why not just use a threshold alarm?" — because one sensor cannot tell a dangerous gas from a harmless one. See page 03.

Chapter 2 — Literature review

About 45 studies in six themes: machine learning for sensor arrays, SVMs for gas, IoT and TinyML detectors, deep learning on this exact dataset, industrial and cold-chain uses, and sensor drift. Table 3 lists earlier results on MultimodalGasData. Section 2.7 states the research gap: the only sensor-only SVM on this data scored 88.36% with no tuning described, and nobody measured real-time speed.

Most likely question

"What is new in your work?" — a full derivation, a properly tuned SVM that beats the earlier SVM by about 10 points, measured latency, and an honest drift test.

Chapter 4 — Results and discussion

Baseline 80.31%; grid search picks \(C=100,\ \gamma=10\); tuned model 98.23%; confusion matrix with only 17 errors, all between NoGas and Perfume; ROC curves; learning curve; kernel comparison (linear 84.79%, polynomial 95.52%, RBF 98.23%); leak view (99.03% sensitivity); latency about 1.1 ms; drift test (54.37%); comparison with earlier studies.

Most likely question

"Why does accuracy drop to 54% in the time-ordered test?" — sensor drift. See page 11.

Chapter 5 — Conclusion

Summary, five contributions, four limitations (lab gases, drift, no ppm calibration, latency measured on a laptop) and five ideas for future work (real industrial gases, drift compensation, support vector regression for concentration, ESP32 deployment, time-based features).

Say this — the ninety-second walkthrough

"Chapter 1 explains why gas leaks in factories and warehouses are dangerous, with real cases from Bangladesh, and why single-sensor threshold alarms give false alarms."

"Chapter 2 reviews about forty-five studies and finds the gap: on this dataset the only sensor-only SVM reached 88%, untuned, and nobody measured speed."

"Chapter 3 derives the SVM fully — margin, dual problem, KKT conditions, RBF kernel and one-vs-one voting — and explains scaling, tuning and the metrics."

"Chapter 4 shows the tuned model reaches 98.23%, decides in about a millisecond, and catches 99% of gas readings, and it honestly shows that sensor drift lowers accuracy on later readings."

"Chapter 5 concludes that a well-tuned, explainable SVM is a practical real-time detector, and lists the next steps: real industrial gases and drift compensation."
