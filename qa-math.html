Q&A — the mathematics

A mathematics panel will test the theory behind the SVM. These are the questions most likely to come, with exact, short answers.

In plain words

Most maths questions on an SVM come down to five ideas: margin, convexity, duality, KKT and kernels. If you can explain each in two sentences and write its key equation, you are well prepared. Derivations are on page 06.

Geometry

M01

Why is the margin equal to \(2/\|\mathbf{w}\|\)?

The distance from a point to the plane \(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}+b=0\) is \(|\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}+b|/\|\mathbf{w}\|\); the closest points have \(|\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}+b|=1\), so each side is \(1/\|\mathbf{w}\|\) and the total gap is \(2/\|\mathbf{w}\|\).

geometry
M02

Why minimise \(\tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|^2\) instead of maximising \(2/\|\mathbf{w}\|\)?

They have the same solution, but \(\tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|^2\) is smooth and convex, which turns the problem into a quadratic programme with one global minimum.

The ½ just makes the derivative \(\mathbf{w}\) instead of \(2\mathbf{w}\).

geometry
M03

Why can we fix the scale so that the closest points give \(|\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{x}+b|=1\)?

Multiplying \(\mathbf{w}\) and \(b\) by any positive number describes the same plane, so we are free to choose the scale that makes the closest value exactly 1.

geometry

Optimisation

M04

Is the SVM problem convex? Why does that matter?

Yes — a convex quadratic objective with linear constraints — so any local minimum is the global minimum and the answer does not depend on where the solver starts.

optimisation
M05

What is the dual problem and why use it?

It rewrites the SVM in terms of the Lagrange multipliers \(\alpha_i\) only; the data then appear only as inner products, which allows the kernel trick, and the constraints become a simple box \(0\le\alpha_i\le C\).

$$\max_{\boldsymbol\alpha}\ \sum_i\alpha_i-\tfrac12\sum_i\sum_j\alpha_i\alpha_jy_iy_jK(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_j),\quad \sum_i\alpha_iy_i=0,\ 0\le\alpha_i\le C$$
optimisationdual
M06

Why do the primal and dual give the same answer?

Strong duality: the problem is convex and Slater's condition holds (the constraints are linear and a feasible point exists), so the duality gap is zero.

optimisationduality
M07

State the KKT conditions for the soft-margin SVM.

Primal feasibility, dual feasibility (\(\alpha_i,\mu_i\ge0\)), stationarity (\(\mathbf{w}=\sum\alpha_iy_i\phi(\mathbf{x}_i)\), \(\sum\alpha_iy_i=0\), \(\alpha_i+\mu_i=C\)) and complementary slackness (\(\alpha_i[y_if(\mathbf{x}_i)-1+\xi_i]=0\), \((C-\alpha_i)\xi_i=0\)).

M10

What does \(C\) do mathematically?

It weights the total slack \(\sum\xi_i\) against \(\tfrac12\|\mathbf{w}\|^2\), and in the dual it is the upper bound on each \(\alpha_i\), limiting how much any single point can pull the boundary.

optimisationC
M11

What is the hinge loss and how does it relate to the SVM?

\(\max\{0,1-yf(\mathbf{x})\}\); at the optimum each \(\xi_i\) equals the hinge loss of point \(i\), so the soft-margin SVM is hinge loss plus an \(L_2\) penalty on \(\mathbf{w}\).

optimisationhinge
M12

How is the dual actually solved?

By SMO: because \(\sum\alpha_iy_i=0\), at least two multipliers must change together, so SMO repeatedly optimises a pair analytically and clips it to \([0,C]\) until the KKT conditions hold.

optimisationsmo

Kernels

M13

What is the kernel trick?

Replacing every inner product \(\phi(\mathbf{x})^\top\phi(\mathbf{z})\) with a kernel \(K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})\), which gives a linear separator in a high-dimensional space — a curved one in the original space — without ever computing \(\phi\).

kernel
M14

What makes a function a valid kernel?

Mercer's condition: it must be symmetric and positive semi-definite, i.e. every Gram matrix satisfies \(\mathbf{c}^\top G\mathbf{c}\ge0\); this keeps the dual convex.

kernelmercer
M15

Explain the RBF kernel and the role of \(\gamma\).

\(K=\exp(-\gamma\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}\|^2)\) is 1 for identical points and decays with distance; \(\gamma\) sets the decay speed — small \(\gamma\) gives smooth, nearly linear boundaries, large \(\gamma\) gives tight, wiggly ones.

Its feature space is infinite-dimensional (expand the exponential as a power series).

kernelrbf
M16

Your best \(\gamma\) is 10. Is that large?

Yes, on standardised data it is quite local: similarity halves at a distance of about \(\sqrt{\ln 2/10}\approx0.26\) standard deviations, so the boundary follows the data closely.

That suits the narrow NoGas/Perfume overlap, but it also profits from near-duplicate neighbouring readings in a random split — one reason the time-ordered test is lower. See page 11.

Multi-class, probabilities, PCA

M17

One-vs-one or one-vs-rest — which and why?

One-vs-one (what LIBSVM uses): \(k(k-1)/2=6\) small, balanced two-class problems, which train faster and avoid the 1:3 imbalance that one-vs-rest creates.

multiclass
M18

How does an SVM give probabilities?

Platt scaling fits a sigmoid \(1/(1+e^{Af+B})\) to the decision values by maximum likelihood on held-out scores; pairwise probabilities are then combined by pairwise coupling.

probability
M19

What does PCA compute?

The eigenvectors of the covariance matrix of the standardised data; each eigenvalue is the variance along its direction, and the first two components here keep 87.8% of the variance.

pca

Statistics and evaluation

M20

Define precision, recall and F1.

Precision \(=TP/(TP+FP)\), recall \(=TP/(TP+FN)\), and F1 is their harmonic mean \(2PR/(P+R)\).

Harmonic mean, because it is low whenever either one is low.

metrics
M21

What does an AUC of 0.9986 mean?

Pick one reading of a class and one of another class at random: 99.86% of the time the model gives the true class the higher probability.

metricsauc
M22

Why cross-validation instead of one validation set for tuning?

Averaging over five different splits gives a more stable estimate, so the chosen \(C,\gamma\) depend less on one lucky or unlucky split.

cv
M23

What is the cost of one prediction?

\(O(n_{SV}\,d)\): one kernel evaluation per support vector, each \(O(d)\) — here about \(1{,}304\times7\), roughly \(10^4\) operations.
