Hard questions & honest gaps

Every project has weak points. A good examiner will find them. The best defence is to know them first, admit them calmly, and show you understand their effect.

In plain words

The 98% result is real, but it was measured under friendly conditions: readings were shuffled randomly, so the test set contains near-twins of training readings. When you tested on later readings, the score fell to 54%. That is the biggest single thing to understand about your project.

The other gaps are about realism: lab gases instead of industrial ones, raw sensor counts instead of ppm, and speed measured on a laptop. None of these is a mistake. They are limits, and you already name them in Chapter 5. Say so with confidence.

The evaluation

W01

Isn't 98% too good? Your readings are 2 seconds apart, so the test set has near-copies of training readings.

Yes, a random split is optimistic for time-series data, which is exactly why I also ran a time-ordered test, and there accuracy was 54.37%.

The 98% measures how well the model recognises the gas patterns under the conditions it was trained on. The 54% measures how it copes with drift into the future. Both are reported in the thesis; earlier studies on this dataset reported only random splits.

Say this

"You're right that neighbouring readings are almost identical, so the random split is the friendly case. That's why Section 4.9 trains on the first 70% of each recording and tests on the last 30%. Accuracy drops to 54%, mostly later readings being classed as Mixture, which is sensor drift. So my claim is: the SVM separates the gas patterns very well, and a deployed system needs recalibration or drift compensation."

leakagecritical
W02

So in practice your model would fail?

A model trained once and never updated would degrade, as any MOS-sensor model does; the remedy is periodic recalibration or retraining, which for an SVM takes seconds.

Drift is a known, open problem for metal-oxide sensors (Vergara et al., 2012). Even dedicated drift methods stay under 90% on the standard drift benchmark (Rehman et al., 2021). Online SVM updating (Dong et al., 2021) is a concrete next step.

drift
W03

Did the large γ = 10 just memorise neighbours?

Partly, yes — a local kernel benefits from near-duplicate neighbours in a random split, which is one reason the time-ordered score is lower.

Cross-validation picked γ = 10 under the same random-split conditions. A fairer tuning would use time-blocked folds; that is a good improvement for future work.

gammaleakage
W04

Why have a validation set if you tuned with cross-validation?

The validation set was only a second, independent check of generalisation (98.12%); tuning used 5-fold cross-validation inside the training set, and the test set was used once.

split
W05

Your results come from a single split and seed. How confident are you?

The cross-validation (97.63%), validation (98.12%) and test (98.23%) scores agree closely, which suggests the estimate is stable; repeated splits with confidence intervals would be a stronger check.

With 960 test readings, a 95% interval for 98.23% is roughly ±0.8 points (\(\pm1.96\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}\)).

statistics
W06

Training accuracy is 99.98%. Isn't that overfitting?

The gap to test accuracy is under two points and validation agrees with test, so it is not harmful overfitting on this data — but the drift test shows it does not transfer well over time.

Realism

W07

The gases are deodorant and incense. How is that "manufacturing and logistics"?

It isn't directly — the dataset is a laboratory benchmark; I used it to develop and validate the method, and real industrial gases are clearly stated as future work.

The method transfers: MQ2, MQ5, MQ6 target LPG and natural gas, MQ7 CO, MQ135 ammonia. Only retraining on real data is needed.

datarealism
W08

Your inputs are ADC counts. Can the model say how much gas there is?

No — the data are uncalibrated, so the model identifies the gas type but not its concentration in ppm; support vector regression on calibrated data would add that.

units
W09

You claim real-time IoT, but you never built a device.

Correct — I measured latency on a laptop CPU (about 1.1 ms) and argued from the prediction cost \(O(n_{SV}d)\) that a microcontroller would still be far under the 2 s deadline; deploying on an ESP32 is future work.

Storage check: 1,304 support vectors × 7 features, plus coefficients, is about 1,304 × 10 numbers ≈ 50 KB as 32-bit floats — small enough for an ESP32's memory.

hardware
W10

Perfume is harmless. Why treat it as "gas present" in the leak view?

In the leak view it stands for "a foreign vapour in the air"; the four-class output still separates it from Smoke and Mixture, so an operator can tell a harmless vapour from a hazardous one.

That is the main advantage over a threshold alarm.

Data and method details

W11

The dataset paper says 90 minutes at 2-second intervals — that is 2,700 readings, not 1,600. Why?

The original authors do not explain it; I used the released file as it is (1,600 per class) and note the discrepancy rather than guessing.

data
W12

There are 228 repeated rows. Why keep them?

They are genuine consecutive readings of a slowly changing signal, not copying errors, so removing them would distort the data.

data
W13

Is the comparison with other papers fair?

Only roughly — they used different splits (70/30 versus my 70/15/15) and single runs, so I treat differences of about a point as equal and claim only the clear gaps, such as 98.23% versus the earlier SVM's 88.36%.

comparison
W14

You used probability=True, which is deprecated in scikit-learn 1.9. Does that matter?

It only affects the probability outputs used for ROC curves and confidence; class predictions come from voting and are unaffected, and the version is stated so the work is reproducible.

Note: predict_proba can occasionally disagree with predict, because one comes from Platt scaling and the other from voting.

software
W15

Why not a random forest, which got 98.67% in another paper?

The goal was a mathematically transparent model I could derive fully; the tuned SVM reaches a comparable 98.23% and adds measured latency — a single-model study was the agreed scope.
